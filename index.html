Math Visualizations

Interactive pictures for selected topics. Change the parameters and watch the mathematics respond; every page keeps its state in the URL, so a link reproduces what you see.

  • Sequences and convergence

    Pick a band of half-width \(\varepsilon\) around the limit \(L\) and find the \(N\) from which every term \(a_n\) stays inside it; shrink \(\varepsilon\) and watch \(N\) grow.

  • ε–δ continuity

    Choose \(\varepsilon\), then search for a \(\delta\) that keeps the graph over \(|x - x_0| < \delta\) inside the band \(f(x_0) \pm \varepsilon\), and see why a jump defeats every \(\delta\).

  • Riemann sums

    How the integral \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\) arises as the limit of rectangle sums over finer and finer partitions, squeezed between lower and upper sums.

  • Secant to tangent

    The derivative as a limit: shrink \(h\) and watch the secant through \(x_0\) and \(x_0 + h\) turn into the tangent, and see where no derivative exists.

  • Taylor polynomials

    Add terms \(\frac{f^{(k)}(x_0)}{k!}(x - x_0)^k\) one by one and watch the polynomial hug \(f\) near \(x_0\), on a stretch that grows with \(n\), or stops growing.

  • Fundamental theorem of calculus

    Slide \(b\) and watch the area \(\int_a^b f\) build the antiderivative \(F\), whose tangent at \(b\) has slope exactly \(f(b)\).

  • Fourier series

    Build a periodic function from sines and cosines, one harmonic at a time, and watch the overshoot at a jump that never goes away (the Gibbs phenomenon).

  • Linear maps in 2D

    Drag the images of \(e_1\) and \(e_2\): they are the columns of the matrix, and they carry the whole grid, the unit square (area \(|\det A|\)) and its orientation with them.

  • Eigenvectors

    Turn a vector \(v\) around the unit circle and watch \(Av\): where it lands on \(v\)'s own line, \(Av = \lambda v\). Real, repeated and complex eigenvalues.

  • Direction fields

    The arrows of \(y' = f(x, y)\) show the slope a solution must have at each point. Click to start a solution curve and watch it follow them.

  • Complex functions

    Color each point \(z\) by the value \(f(z)\): hue for the argument, lightness for the modulus. Zeros turn black, poles white, and their order shows as how often the colors wind around.

  • Central limit theorem

    Average \(n\) random draws, again and again: whatever the distribution, the histogram of the means approaches a normal curve of spread \(\sigma/\sqrt{n}\).

  • Stereographic projection

    The line from the north pole through a point \(P\) of the sphere meets the plane in \(z\): the sphere without \(N\) becomes \(\mathbb{C}\), and \(N\) becomes \(\infty\).

  • Riemann zeta function

    \(\zeta(x) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k^x}\) for real \(x > 1\), and how the partial sums \(1 + \frac{1}{2^x} + \dots + \frac{1}{n^x}\) creep up to it as \(n\) grows.

  • Exponential functions

    \(f(x) = a\,e^{bx+c} + d\): what each parameter does, tangents that always meet the asymptote \(1/b\) away, growth and decay.

  • Surfaces \(z = f(x, y)\)

    A 3D plotter with slices, tangent planes, geodesics and their acceleration vectors.
